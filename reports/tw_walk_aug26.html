Buffers hang off the cutoff, not off the global range. An earlier version of this page used 6.29 and 14.16, taken from the ceiling of the global EM size range with the 1.8× light-rebalancing multiple. Both were wrong: GIMI §3.1.5.1 defines the buffers as 2/3 of and 1.5 times the Market Size-Segment Cutoff, which is this market's own number.

The FIF column is not one number from one place. MSCI factsheet is a float cap read straight off a published top-ten and MSCI weights is inverted from the index weight — both are effectively MSCI's own figure. TDCC is Taiwan depository holdings scaled to that overlap. Yahoo is a third-party estimate and carries the most error. Hover any cell for the definition.

Which source a name has changes how much to trust its position. Most of the universe is on the estimated tier, so a company sitting within a few per cent of the cutoff, the floor or the addition bar can cross it on float error alone — and the four called additions all sit far enough above the bar that the tier does not decide them.
