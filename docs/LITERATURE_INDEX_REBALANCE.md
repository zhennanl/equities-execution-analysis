## 1. The classics — the index effect exists (1986-2011)

| Paper | Finding |
|---|---|
| Shleifer (1986), "Do Demand Curves for Stocks Slope Down?" [cite] | S&P 500 additions jump ~+3% at announcement with no new information — demand curves slope down; the founding result |
| Harris & Gurel (1986) [cite] | Same-size pop but argues PRICE PRESSURE: the effect partially REVERSES once index demand is filled — the reversal literature our completion-leg design descends from |
| Lynch & Mendenhall (1997) [cite] | Drift between announcement and effective day + partial post-effective reversal — the anatomy of the window we measure |
| Wurgler & Zhuravskaya (2002) [cite] | Stocks without close substitutes jump more — arbitrage limits size the effect |
| Chen, Noronha & Singal (2004) ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4992686_The_Price_Response_to_SP_500_Index_Additions_and_Deletions_Evidence_of_Asymmetry_and_a_New_Explanation)) | ASYMMETRY: adds permanent-ish, deletes largely reverse — investor-awareness explanation |
| Petajisto (2011), "The index premium and its hidden cost" ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227415032_The_index_premium_and_its_hidden_cost_for_index_funds)) | 1990-2005: adds +8.8%, deletes −15.1% around events; the index premium costs S&P 500 trackers ~21-28 bp/yr — rebalance drag as a fund cost |
| Madhavan (2003), "The Russell Reconstitution Effect" ([PDF](https://www.hillsdaleinv.com/uploads/The_Russell_Reconstitution_Effect,_Ananth_Madhaven,_Financial_Analysts_Journal,_JulyAugust_2003,_Pages_51-64.pdf)) | Russell June reconstitution generates large predictable price/liquidity effects; early institutional-grade treatment |

## 2. The modern turn — the US effect is disappearing

| Paper | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Greenwood & Sammon, "The Disappearing Index Effect"** (J. Finance, Apr 2025; [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4294297), [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)) | S&P 500 add abnormal return fell from **+7.4% (1990s) to +0.3% (2010s)**; deletes similarly to −0.1% — DESPITE passive share rising. Drivers: predictability + arbitrageur front-running + migration mechanics. THE reference for "the easy trade is gone in the US" |
| Bennett, Stulz & Wang (NBER w27593, [PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27593/w27593.pdf)) | "Does joining the S&P 500 hurt firms?" — modern inclusion effects near zero / possibly negative |
| NY Fed staff report 484 ([PDF](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr484.pdf)) | "Is there an S&P 500 index effect?" — skeptical modern re-measurement |

## 5. Where OUR measurements sit against this literature

- **We work where the effect still LIVES.** Greenwood-Sammon's
  disappearance is an S&P 500 (US mega-cap) result. Our measured
  Asia effects are alive: TW adds +329 bps window drift, CN/HK
  announcement pops of 1,000+ bps, MSCI-delete T-multiples 16-38x.
  Consistent with their own mechanism: effects fade where
  predictability + arb capital saturate — US first, Asia later.
- **Price pressure + reversal (Harris-Gurel) is our completion-leg
  design** — measured ~50% reversal by T+5 on our events.
- **The add/delete asymmetry (Chen et al.) shows up inverted by
  event class in our windows** — FTSE-class deletes recover into
  the print; MSCI-class press through it: the literature's
  asymmetry is not one number, it is class-conditional.
- **Chinco-Sammon's 3.15x excess volume is our auction
  concentration** — we measure the same phenomenon per-name
  (44-71% auction shares) and market-wide (25% of TW's value in
  one print).
- **Front-running (practitioner consensus + G-S mechanism) is our
  crowding layer, quantified** — short builds into events, the
  CONSENSUS/UNPRICED distinction, and the A+3 momentum/reversal
  split by event class.
- **What we add that the literature mostly lacks:** point-in-time
  prediction GRADING at name level (17/17 adds), official-data
  window microstructure outside the US, and execution
  counterfactuals conditioned on PIT-legal signals.
