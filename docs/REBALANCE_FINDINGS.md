# Taiwan index-rebalance findings

Generated by `scripts/rebalance_analysis.py` from `data/rebalance_analysis.json`. Every figure below is interpolated from that file; nothing here is typed by hand.

## The sample, before anything else

- **136 analysable windows** (58 additions, 78 deletions), 2015–2026, Taiwan only.
- 176 windows are priced, but 40 carry an **estimated announcement date** and are excluded from every event-time number. Day 0 is the pre-news baseline; on those windows it is 2–7 sessions wrong, which is larger than the effect being measured.
- Returns are **excess over TAIEX**, close to close.
- Taiwan is priced from TWSE/TPEx day files, which retain delisted companies, so the deletion sample is **survivor-safe**. This is the honest side of the add/delete asymmetry question.

## The six numbers that matter

| | additions | deletions |
|---|---|---|
| drift, ann+1 → eff−1 (median) | +1.80% | -2.12% |
| …and how often it has the right sign | 62% | 64% |
| …interquartile range | -3.00% to +16.33% | -6.56% to +2.10% |
| effective-day print, × ADV (median) | 6.5x | 19.7x |
| …p90 | 14.8x | 38.2x |
| effective-day move (median) | +0.66% | -0.95% |

**The mean is not the median and the gap is the point.** Addition drift averages +5.79% against a median of +1.80%. The distribution is heavily right-skewed — Yageo in Nov-2017, Wistron in May-2023, Walsin in May-2018 — so a book sized on the mean is sized on three events.

## A3 — Taiwan has no non-events

Zero of 136 events satisfy *vol_mult_eff < 2 AND |total_alpha| < 1%*. Only 9 windows print under 2× ADV at all. An earlier China cut ran 61% non-events; Taiwan runs 0%. **Every MSCI Taiwan change is a trade.**

## C3 — the schedule question, answered twice

| schedule | median saved vs eff close | tracking-error contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 100% at the effective close | +0.00% | 0 (benchmark) |
| 25% × last four days | +1.01% | 2.37% |
| 100% at ann+1 | +3.74% | 10.98% |

**For the tracker the right column is the answer.** The effective close is not a good execution of the benchmark, it *is* the benchmark, so every alternative buys P&L with tracking error. **For the pod the left column is the answer** and the right column is the risk budget.

## C4 / H5 — is the trade getting crowded?

Capture — the share of the move still available after the announcement gap — has a median of 0.77 on additions and 0.74 on deletions. Roughly three quarters of the move is still there the morning after.

And it is **not** decaying: capture ran 0.57 in 2015 and 0.91 in 2026. Pre-announcement drift *has* grown (|pre_drift| median 5.3% → 14.7%), so the market is anticipating more — but it is not taking the post-announcement move away. On the bank's own test for crowding, **the answer is no**, and that is the opposite of what the question expected.

*(n per year is 4–21; treat the year-on-year path as EXPLORATORY and the endpoints as the claim.)*

## G1 / G3 — where the pod's exit lives

Additions give back -4.05% in the five sessions after the effective close — more than the median drift they earned. Deletions revert +0.81%.

The ann+1 → effective-close round trip is +3.69% gross with a 68% hit rate, and +3.29% at a 40bp round-trip cost. **The edge survives cost; the dispersion is what needs sizing** — p10 is -8.49%.

## J4 — size on the excursion, not the outcome

A position entered at ann+1 and held to the effective close is under water by -3.03% at its worst point on the median addition, and -9.61% at the tenth percentile. The final number is not the number the risk manager sees.

## N3 — the borrow signal does not work

Borrow balance into a deletion builds to 1.30× its level 35 days earlier (n=62). It does **not** predict the effective-day move: rank correlation -0.023 on n=62.

This is a negative result and it is worth more than a fitted curve would have been. A crowded short into a Taiwanese deletion is not, on this evidence, a squeeze signal. The bank asks whether each flow dataset earns its place in the pitch — on this test, borrow does not.
