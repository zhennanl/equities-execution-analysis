# Questions for Her — Curated & Ranked

*July 2026. What makes an interview question high-quality: it can't be
googled, it demonstrates a working model of the job, it invites HER expertise
(people enjoy answering questions only they can answer), and her answer tells
you something you actually need. Prepare six, ask two or three. The follow-up
you ask to HER answer makes the impression — not the opening question.*

## Delivery rules

1. **Two to three questions, chosen live** — drop any that the conversation
   already answered; referencing something she said earlier ("you mentioned
   X — does that also apply to…") beats every prepared question.
2. **Follow up once** on her answer before moving to your next question.
   A question fired and abandoned reads as a checklist; a question pursued
   reads as a colleague.
3. **Sequence:** one craft question → one product/impact question → the
   closer. Save the personal-arc question for genuine rapport only.
4. Note her answers — they're the raw material for the thank-you note and
   for round three.

## Tier 1 — ask these if nothing better emerges live

**1. The craft question.**
*"When a client's own numbers disagree with your TCA — their wheel says one
thing, your analysis says another — how do those conversations actually
resolve? Is it usually data, methodology, or framing?"*
Why it works: it names the job's central friction, invites a war story, and
signals you already know the work is reconciliation, not reporting.
Listen for: whether the team leads with more data or better framing — that's
her working style, and your thank-you note can echo it.

**2. The impact-loop question.**
*"What's a recent example of the team's analysis actually changing the algo
product — and what made the case persuasive internally?"*
Why it works: the second clause is the quality marker — it asks about the
POLITICS of evidence, which is the real skill of the spec-proposal
responsibility in her JD. Juniors ask what the team does; colleagues ask
what makes it land.
Listen for: the evidence bar (does R&D demand experiments? backtests?) —
this tells you how your A/B instincts will be received.

**3. The closer.**
*"What would a great first ninety days from me look like — and what's the
first thing you'd want taken off your plate?"*
Why it works: concrete, hire-shaped, and the second half converts the
abstract role into her actual backlog. Her answer is effectively a job
offer's table of contents.
Listen for: whether the answer is analysis-shaped or relationship-shaped —
calibrates what to emphasize in any final round.

## Tier 2 — strong substitutes, situation-dependent

**4. The Tokyo-arc question** *(only if her Japan history has come up, or
she's clearly enjoying the conversation).*
*"You were on the desk through the tick-size program era in Tokyo — does
living through one structural change like that shape how you're approaching
the 2024 close reform with clients now?"*
Why it works: connects her professional history to a live market event,
respectfully — it invites reflection, which senior people rarely get asked
for. This is the single most memorable question available to you.
Risk: reads as over-researched if rapport is thin. Judge in the moment.

**5. The customization-boundary question.**
*"How does the team decide when a client customization should become a
product default versus stay bespoke? Is there a threshold of evidence, or
is it case by case?"*
Why it works: sits exactly on the JD's advisory↔product boundary and implies
you understand customization at scale is a product-management problem.

**6. The measurement-culture question.**
*"When a client wants a wheel verdict faster than the sample honestly
allows, how does the team handle that pressure?"*
Why it works: it's the statistics-first signal (round-2 feedback!) posed as
a client-management reality — the intersection is precisely her seat.
Listen for: whether 'not yet separable' is a sayable sentence there. Her
answer tells you the team's actual statistical integrity.

**7. The AI-that-stuck question** *(if your AI strength discussion landed).*
*"Where has AI genuinely stuck in the team's daily workflow so far — and
where did it disappoint? As the drafting and triage get automated, where do
you want analysts spending the recovered hours?"*
Why it works: grounded (stuck vs disappointed invites honesty, not PR), and
the second half is the hybrid-workforce question seniors are actively
thinking about — you're offering her a chance to think aloud.

## The optional bold closer (use only if the interview went well)

**8.** *"Is there anything in my background that gives you hesitation for
this seat — something I could address directly now?"*
Why it works: it surfaces objections while you're still in the room, and
confidence-with-openness is itself evidence for a client-facing role.
Risk: don't pair it with #3 — one closer only.

## Do NOT ask

- Anything on the public website or in the JD ("what does the team do?").
- Compensation, hours, WFH — not at this round.
- Generic culture questions ("how would you describe the culture?") — wasted
  slots that signal no model of the job.
- Anything that quizzes HER ("do you use Almgren-Chriss?") — questions that
  test the interviewer always misfire.
- More than one flattery-adjacent personal question — one Tokyo-arc maximum.
